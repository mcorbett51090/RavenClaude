# Purchasing-Cooperative Interview Guide — P0a

**Purpose:** 2–4 interviews with purchasing cooperatives across the candidate states, per plan.md P0a
step 7 (CE12). Feeds directly into the TEC pre-mortem (step 5) and the "no incumbent already serves this"
exit condition (P0a acceptance test (e)).

**Target time:** 30–45 minutes. These are more consultative/professional conversations than the buyer
interviews — co-op staff are used to talking about their own programs and will often volunteer more than
you ask for if you let them.

---

## 0. Before the call

- Identify whether this co-op runs (or has run) anything like TEC, and whether it's a public agency,
  a nonprofit, or something else — this affects both the licensing question and the future
  public-lead-agency question below.
- Know roughly what share of the target product(s)' market this co-op plausibly touches (state size,
  member count) so you can sanity-check their answer to the spend-share question.

## 1. Intro (2 min)

> "Thanks for the time. I'm researching whether an independent, buyer-side price benchmark for school
> [SaaS category] purchases would be useful alongside — or instead of — what co-ops already offer. I'd
> like to understand how much of this spend already flows through cooperative agreements, and whether a
> benchmark like this is something a co-op like yours would build or would want to license from someone
> else."

## 2. Core questions

### A. Share of spend already through co-op channels

- "Roughly what share of your member districts' spend on [product/category] flows through your
  cooperative agreements versus direct-to-vendor or another co-op?"
- "Do you track effective unit pricing across your membership for this category, or mainly total
  contract value?"
- "How many vendors/contracts do you carry in this category, and how often do they turn over?"

### B. Do they plan their own buyer-side benchmark?

- "Have you built, or considered building, your own pricing benchmark or comparison tool for members —
  something that shows a member 'here's what similar districts are paying'?"
- If yes: "What's stopped it from shipping, or what stage is it at?" (This is a direct competitive-risk
  signal — if a co-op is already close to shipping this, that changes P0a's read on incumbents.)
- If no: "What's held you back — data, staffing, legal concerns, or it just hasn't come up as a
  priority?"

### C. Would they license one from Buying Power?

- "If an independent group built this and kept it vendor-neutral, would your organization ever license
  or white-label it for your members, rather than building it yourselves?"
- "What would that need to look like — data provenance, methodology transparency, price point — to be
  something you'd put your name behind?"
- "Would you want exclusivity in your state/region, or would a shared, cross-co-op benchmark still be
  useful to you?"

### D. TEC pre-mortem probes (F11 — ask only if relevant to this co-op's region/history)

- "Are you familiar with TEC(the co-op referenced in our research as C16/C19)? What's your read on why
  it [succeeded/struggled/shut down] — was it more a demand problem, a funding problem, or something
  structural about the co-op model?"
- "Who typically ends up paying a co-op's membership or admin fee in your experience — the vendor, the
  district, or a blended model?"

### E. Future public-lead-agency partnership (tactful, exploratory — do not oversell)

Frame this as genuine long-range scouting, not a near-term ask. A private company cannot award a
bid-exempt master agreement to public districts on its own; a future phase of this work might need a
public lead agency or cooperative partner to do that legally. This is early enough that you're only
testing the water, not proposing anything concrete.

- "Longer-term — not something I'm asking about today — if an independent group like ours ever wanted to
  offer districts a cooperative purchasing agreement built on this pricing data, is that a role your
  organization could ever see itself playing as the public lead agency, or is that outside what you'd
  consider?"
- "What would you need to see from us — track record, data quality, member demand — before that
  conversation would even be worth having?"
- If they show real interest: "Would it be OK if I followed up down the line, once we have more evidence
  this is worth pursuing, rather than asking you to commit to anything now?"

Do not push past a light "yes, worth a future conversation" / "no, not something we do" answer — this is
a two-question probe embedded in a data-collection interview, not a partnership pitch.

## 3. Closing

- "Is there anyone else at another co-op, in another state, you'd suggest I talk to for this?"
- "Would you be open to a follow-up once we have a draft benchmark methodology, to sanity-check it
  against what you see across your membership?"
- Thank them, confirm how they'd like to be attributed (or not) in any summary.

## 4. Post-call logging

Record: co-op name/state, estimated % of category spend through the co-op, existing/planned benchmark
(Y/N + stage), licensing interest (Y/N/maybe), TEC read (if applicable — cause established / indirect
evidence / no evidence, per plan.md's three-state finding), public-lead-agency interest signal
(cold/lukewarm/warm).
