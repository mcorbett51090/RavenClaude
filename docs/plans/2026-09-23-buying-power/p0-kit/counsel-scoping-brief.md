## N1 — what we need answered (narrow check)

1. **Legality of our harvest method.** We plan to collect public-record contract data three ways:
   formal public-records requests, capturing publicly-posted board packets, and (where a district's own
   check register is posted) reading published financial disclosures. Is there any legal risk difference
   between these three methods — e.g., do a district or vendor's website terms-of-use limit automated
   collection of publicly posted board packets?

2. **Republication and Terms-of-Service exposure.** Once we've collected this data, can we show it back
   to member districts (and non-member districts, if relevant) at the record level — i.e., showing an
   individual district's actual contract terms, not just an aggregate — without exposure? Please address
   member vs. non-member display separately.

3. **A per-state citation for our launch state.** We need the specific, cited statute or rule governing
   whether/how contract pricing obtained via public records may be used and republished (the
   `records_pricing_public` rule) for our launch state. `[unverified — plan.md flags this as needing a
   specific citation confirmed by counsel; do not proceed on an assumed citation]`.

4. **A cease-and-desist response playbook.** A pre-written, ready-to-use response plan for the scenario
   where a vendor or district sends us a cease-and-desist letter over our data collection or publication.

5. **Records egress for the artifacts we deliver.** If we produce a written brief or price-reasonableness
   memo and give it to a district, is that memo itself then a public record subject to that district's
   own disclosure obligations? Specifically:
   - Whether such delivered briefs/memos are themselves public information under the launch state's
     public-records law once in the district's possession (analogous provisions to Texas Government
     Code §552.002 and §552.104 — confirm the actual launch-state citation; the Texas cites are
     illustrative from our planning doc, not a claim about the launch state `[unverified — confirm
     launch-state citation]`).
   - Whether a negotiation-period exception exists in the launch state (analogous to Texas §552.104) that
     would let a district temporarily withhold our memo while negotiating a purchase, and when any such
     exception expires.
   - **All of this should be treated as unverified until this memo exists** — we are not currently relying
     on any assumption about egress protection, and we understand that if the memo turns out unprotected,
     our delivered artifacts should be treated as public by default from day one.

6. **Price-reasonableness memo language.** Draft or review boilerplate for a "this price appears
   reasonable/unreasonable relative to comparable public contracts" memo, addressing:
   - Compliance with the cost-reasonableness standard for grant-funded purchases under 2 CFR 200.320 and
     200.324 (federal Uniform Guidance procurement standards) — how our memo language should be worded so
     a district can rely on it in that context.
   - Appropriate methodology disclaimers (statistical basis, sample size, what the memo does and does not
     certify).

**Explicitly out of scope for N1:** the antitrust buyer-side information-exchange analysis — that's L1,
scoped separately below.

## L1 — what we need scoped (not answered yet)

We are building a benchmark from *pooled buyer-side pricing data* — i.e., aggregating what multiple
school districts (and potentially private schools) pay for the same SaaS products, then showing
comparisons back to buyers. Before we build or rely on this at scale, we need the full guardrails:

- What are the antitrust risks specific to buyer-side (as opposed to seller-side/competitor) information
  pooling and exchange in this context?
- What design choices (aggregation thresholds, time lags, who sees record-level vs. aggregate data)
  materially reduce that risk?
- **Please also address, or tell us the current state of:** any recent DOJ/FTC guidance or enforcement
  posture specifically on buyer-side data pooling/collaboration — our own research notes this as an area
  we have not yet nailed down and flags it as something requiring a current check with counsel, not an
  assumption based on older guidance.

**What we want from you right now is a scope + cost/time estimate for producing that full analysis** —
not the analysis itself. We will decide separately whether/when to commission the full L1 memo based on
P0a's results.
