# Buyer Interview Guide — P0a

**Purpose:** 20–25 interviews total (~15 public: CTO/CFO/purchasing/procurement; 5–10 private-school or
private-org equivalents) to test demand, willingness to pay, data-sharing willingness, timing, security
expectations, and the board-packet memo wedge, per plan.md P0a step 8 and the exit-criteria in step (c).

**Target time:** 30–40 minutes. Conversational — use this as a checklist you keep in your head, not a
form you read aloud.

**What "done" looks like for this instrument:** at least 8 of ~15 public buyers name a specific renewal
in the next 12 months where they'd use a benchmark, and at least 5 non-binding pilot LOIs (2+ private,
conditional on L1) — see plan.md P0a acceptance test (c). This guide is built to surface both cleanly.

---

## 0. Before the call

- Know which product(s) this person actually buys or has bought (from your contract sample or a
  LinkedIn/district-site check) — don't make them explain their own stack from scratch.
- Have the one-paragraph pitch ready in your own words, e.g.: "I'm exploring whether school districts
  would use an independent, buyer-side price benchmark for [product category] — built from public
  contract data — before signing or renewing. I'm not selling anything yet; I'm trying to find out if
  this is a real problem worth building for."
- Decide up front whether this is a "public" or "private" contact — the confidentiality question (§3
  below) only applies to private/independent-school contacts, since public records are public by
  default.

## 1. Intro & consent script (2 min)

> "Thanks for making time. I'm Matt — I'm doing early research on whether a price-benchmarking tool for
> school [SaaS category] purchases would actually be useful, before I build anything. This isn't a sales
> call and there's no product to buy today. I'd like to ask about how you evaluate and renew contracts,
> and I'll take notes — is it OK if I quote you anonymously in a summary I use to decide whether to build
> this? I won't use your name or district name without checking with you first. Anything you'd rather
> keep off the record, just say so."

Wait for an actual yes. If they hesitate on notes, offer to not record specifics and just summarize
themes.

## 2. Warm-up (3–5 min)

- "Walk me through how you currently decide whether a renewal price is fair — what do you actually look
  at?"
- "When you last renewed [product], what info did you have going in — last year's invoice, a peer's
  price, a rep's word?"
- "Who else is in the room for a decision like that — board, superintendent, business office?"

Listen for: whether they *already* benchmark informally (calls colleagues, asks a co-op, guesses), which
tells you how big a gap this fills.

## 3. Core questions, by topic

### A. Specific upcoming renewal (the core demand test)

This is the single most important question in the guide — it directly feeds acceptance test (c).

- "Do you have a specific renewal coming up in the next 12 months — for [product] or something similar —
  where knowing 'is this price normal' would actually change how you negotiate or what you'd ask for?"
  - Push for specificity: which product, roughly when, and what they'd do differently with a number in
    hand. A vague "sure, that'd be useful" does not count — you want a named renewal.
- "If I sent you a one-page report showing where your quote sits against similar districts' actual
  contracts before that renewal, would you use it? Who would you show it to?"

### B. Willingness to pay

- "If this existed as a paid report or subscription, what would feel like a reasonable price for one
  benchmark memo? For an ongoing subscription across your whole SaaS portfolio?"
- Offer 2–3 anchor tiers if they're stuck (e.g., "somewhere between a one-time report for a few hundred
  dollars, and an annual subscription in the low thousands, covering multiple products") — don't lead,
  but don't leave them with nothing to react to.
- "Would this come out of your own budget line, or would it need board/superintendent sign-off as a new
  purchase?"

### C. Data-sharing willingness (private orgs — skip for public buyers, whose contracts are already public)

- "Would you be willing to share your own contract with us — quantity, price, term — under an NDA, used
  only to build the benchmark, never shown to anyone in a way that identifies you?"
- "What would make you comfortable with that — anonymization, aggregation with N other districts before
  anything is shown, a specific minimum group size before a number is published?"
- "Do your vendor contracts have confidentiality clauses that would block you from sharing pricing at
  all? Have you ever checked with counsel on that?"

### D. Seasonality / timing

- "When in the year do these renewal decisions actually get made — is there a budget cycle or
  fiscal-year-end crunch that drives timing?"
- "Is there a point where a vendor's discount changes based on when you sign — e.g., pressure to sign
  before fiscal year-end?" *(This maps to C48's spend-it-or-lose-it dynamic — don't lead with the term,
  just ask if they've noticed the pattern.)*

### E. Security-questionnaire expectations

- "If you were going to trust a report or a subscription like this with data about your own contracts,
  what would you expect from us on security — SOC 2, a specific questionnaire, insurance?"
- "Has a vendor's SOC 2 status (or lack of it) ever stalled or killed a purchase for you?"

### F. The board-packet memo wedge (F2, path a)

- "If we produced a one-page memo — 'this price is/isn't in line with comparable districts,
  methodology attached' — would you attach that to your board packet as backup for a purchase decision?"
- "What would the memo need to say, or NOT say, for your board or business office to accept it as
  credible? (e.g., does it need a specific disclaimer, a citation to your state's procurement rules, a
  named source for the underlying data?)"
- "Has your board or superintendent ever asked you to justify a price and you didn't have anything to
  point to?"

## 4. Closing ask — LOI interest (3–5 min)

- "Based on what we've talked about — if this existed in a working form in the next few months, would
  you be willing to sign a non-binding letter of interest, just saying you'd pilot it if it's useful?
  No cost, no commitment, just 'we'd take a look.'"
- If yes: "Great — I'll follow up with something short you can sign. Is [name/email] the right contact,
  or should it go to someone else — business office, superintendent?"
- If no or hesitant: "No worries — can I ask what would need to be true for that to make sense?" (This
  answer is often more useful than the yes.)
- "Is there anyone else — another district, a private school, a co-op contact — you think I should talk
  to?" (snowball sampling toward the 20–25 target)

## 5. Wrap-up

- Thank them, confirm anonymity preference, confirm whether they're open to a follow-up call once you
  have a draft memo or report to show them.
- Immediately after the call, log: named renewal (Y/N + product + rough date), WTP tier signal, data
  co-op willingness (private only), SOC 2 mentioned unprompted (Y/N), board-packet memo reaction
  (positive/neutral/negative), LOI interest (Y/N/maybe).
